2. Where the numbers come from

The video gives no pan size. Its batch — 1 lb pasta, 28 oz dairy, 40 oz cheese — is roughly 3½ quarts and is shot in a 9×13. Your pan is bigger in every direction, so the batch scales up 1½× and lands about 2¼ in deep, leaving a half inch for the boil and the broil.

130 in² is the half-pan footprint (12¾ × 10⅜), not the 60 in² quarter pan. Volume is footprint × depth.

4. Method · every container gets used, in order

  1. Set a large pot of water on high with a handful of salt. It should taste like the ocean; you are dropping 1½ lb of pasta in it.
  2. Oven to 350°F, rack middle. Get out your pan.
  3. Ramekin → pot. Low heat. Melt the butter and dump Ramekin 2 (half the seasoning) straight in. It seizes and sizzles for 30 seconds; stir it constantly and do not let it brown.
  4. Flour → pot. Add the 4½ tbsp and stir on low 4 to 6 minutes, until it smells like toasted bread instead of raw flour. It will look like wet sand.
  5. Pitcher → pot. Switch to a whisk. Stream in about a third of the milk-and-cream, whisking hard as it thickens and turns glossy, then the rest. All at once and it goes loose and runny.
  6. Ramekin 1 → pot, then the Dijon. Whisk in the rest of Ramekin 1, then the 1½ tbsp mustard. Low to medium-low, never a boil.
  7. Bowl A → pot. A handful at a time, each melted before the next. Keep the heat low or the cheese breaks and turns grainy. Too thick? Splash of milk.
  8. Cavatappi → pot → pan. By now the water is boiling: cook 8 to 9 minutes, drain and do not rinse (7 minutes if baking tomorrow), fold into the sauce, then half into the pan. Bowl B over it, remaining pasta, then the rest of Bowl B. Level the top; it should sit about 2¼ in up the side.
  9. Pan → oven. 350°F for 30 to 35 minutes, edges bubbling and center set, then broil 2 minutes. Rest 5 minutes, scatter the chives, cut and serve.

6. Notes worth reading

  • Why 1½× and not 2×. Your pan is 6.2 qt empty. The batch needs to finish around 5 qt so it bubbles up rather than over, and 1½× the video lands there: about 5.2 qt, or 2¼ in of a 2¾ in well. Doubling would put 7 qt in a 6.2 qt pan.
  • Watch the salt. Cheese brings a lot; 1 tbsp Diamond Crystal is right, the same spoon of Morton's is noticeably salty. Season the sauce, then taste before the pasta goes in.
  • Cavatappi, not cavatelli. The video says cavatelli, but that is a short solid shell; the pasta on screen is a spiral tube, which is cavatappi (cellentani). Buy the tube — the hollow core is where the cream sits. That swap is why the cream is 2½ cups here instead of 3: cavatappi does not coil up to fill the pan the way cavatelli does, so more sauce is extra liquid in the middle, and the tubes already carry plenty. Sauce still reads creamy, not runny.
  • Do not substitute condensed milk. Evaporated is unsweetened and reduced; condensed is sweetened and roughly a third sugar.
  • Grainy sauce is heat, not the recipe. Cheese goes in off-boil, low, a handful at a time.
  • The pan is 2¾ in deep, so the middle takes longer than a 9×13 bake. If the center still sloshes at 35 min, give it 5 more; if the top darkens, tent with foil.
  • Broiler is the last 2 minutes only, and it goes from crust to carbon fast. Stand at the oven.
  • Make-ahead: boil 7 minutes, assemble, refrigerate covered up to 24 hours. Add 10 minutes to the bake, covered with foil for the first half.
  • Reheat portions at 350°F covered with foil, splash of milk on top.
